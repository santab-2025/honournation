Cybersecurity fundamentals

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting systems, networks, applications, devices and information from unauthorized access, disruption, alteration or destruction.

🛡

Confidentiality

Only authorized people or systems should be able to access sensitive information.

✓

Integrity

Information should remain accurate and protected from unauthorized modification.

⚡

Availability

Authorized users should be able to access systems and information when they need them.

Know the risks

Common cyber threats

Learning how attacks work at a high level helps people recognize and prevent them.

✉

Phishing

Fraudulent messages designed to trick people into revealing credentials, opening malicious content or transferring money.

🔒

Ransomware

Malicious software can make data or systems unavailable and may demand payment from victims.

🧩

Vulnerabilities

Weaknesses in software, configurations or processes can create opportunities for unauthorized access.

👤

Credential Theft

Stolen passwords or session information can allow attackers to impersonate legitimate users.

🌐

Social Engineering

Attackers manipulate people rather than technology to obtain information or cause an unsafe action.

📡

Unsafe Networks

Poorly secured networks can expose devices and communications to interception or unwanted access.

Build good habits

Practical defense

Security is a process. Small habits can dramatically reduce everyday risk.

01

Use strong, unique passwords

Use long passwords or passphrases and avoid reusing the same password across important accounts.

02

Turn on multi-factor authentication

MFA adds another verification factor, making stolen passwords less useful by themselves.

03

Update your software

Keep operating systems, browsers, applications and security tools updated to receive security fixes.

04

Back up important data

Maintain reliable backups so accidental deletion, hardware failure or malware does not destroy your only copy.

05

Verify before you trust

Check links, senders, requests for money and unexpected login prompts before taking action.

Responsible security: Only test systems you own or have explicit permission to assess. Learn in legal labs and controlled environments.
